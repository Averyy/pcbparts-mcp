Privacy Policy

Effective November 22, 2026

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no cookies, no ads, no analytics trackers.
  • We use what your AI assistant sends a tool (search text, part numbers, filters) to answer that call. We keep text from searches and lookups that found nothing or failed for 7 days to fix search.
  • We count popular search words once enough different callers use them. Usage counts and diagnostic records are private.
  • Your IP address is used for rate limiting and abuse prevention. When you use us through a hosted AI app such as ChatGPT or claude.ai, the address we see is that company's server, not yours. Our logs keep it only in hourly summaries of Mouser and DigiKey lookups and of requests turned away by our rate limit, and it is never stored in our usage statistics.
  • To answer some calls our server sends part numbers or search text, never your IP address, to JLCPCB, LCSC, EasyEDA, Mouser, DigiKey or SamacSys. Links in our answers open providers' sites; short datasheet links pass through this server and redirect there.
  • We don't sell or share data for advertising, and we don't use it to train AI models.

Who we are and what this covers

PCB Parts MCP is an independent, open-source project run by Avery L. ("we", "us"). This policy covers the hosted service we run: the website at pcbparts.dev and the MCP server at https://pcbparts.dev/mcp, including when you use it through a connector in Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor or any other MCP client.

The source code is public, so you can check everything described here. If you or your organization run your own copy of the server, that copy is not covered by this policy; whoever runs it is responsible for it.

What we collect and why

1. Tool call contents

When your AI assistant calls one of our tools, it sends the arguments for that call: for example a search query such as 10uF 0805 capacitor, a part number such as C2040 or STM32F103C8T6, filters, or a board or sensor name. We use them to answer calls, reuse cached answers, understand usage and fix search problems, as described below. We don't receive your conversation, your files, or your AI assistant's memory, only the arguments your assistant chooses to send, which can include text it takes from them. Please don't put personal information in part searches.

Parts of some answers are cached to keep the service fast and to stay within our suppliers' rate limits. Caches are keyed by the part number or search text and contain no IP address or other identifier:

  • JLCPCB, LCSC and EasyEDA answers are reused for up to 24 hours. They are held in memory only, so a server restart clears them.
  • Mouser and DigiKey answers (stock, prices and lifecycle) are stored on our server's disk by part number and reused for up to 3 days, whether or not the part was found. They hold only the part numbers asked and the distributors' answers.
  • Lifecycle statuses that Mouser and DigiKey state (for example active, not recommended for new designs, or obsolete) are stored on disk by manufacturer and part number for up to 90 days (a year when the status is obsolete), as are their answers that they don't list a part or give no usable status for it, and used to answer anyone who asks about that part. Each holds the part number, the manufacturer, the status, the distributor's wording and when it was checked.
  • SamacSys ComponentSearchEngine search text and results, and KiCad files, are stored on our server's disk: searches for up to one year (a week where some parts had no model), KiCad files with no end date. We also keep the part IDs and part numbers of models that couldn't be downloaded, until their files are stored. None of it holds addresses.
  • EasyEDA answers about whether a part has a footprint are stored on disk with no fixed end date, since they rarely change. Each holds the part number, the answer, EasyEDA's symbol and footprint IDs, and when it was checked.
  • EasyEDA footprint pad positions, used to compare a replacement part's footprint with the original's, are stored on disk by footprint ID with no fixed end date, since footprints rarely change.

Storing these answers means the same lookup doesn't have to be repeated.

If a lookup fails, the error log line may include the part number or search text involved, so we can fix the problem.

2. IP address

  • Rate limiting: we count requests per IP address (for IPv6, per /64 block, the addresses that share their first 64 bits) over a rolling one-minute window, in memory only, so one caller can't overload the shared service. Addresses in the AI platforms' published address ranges (Anthropic's and OpenAI's public lists), which many of their users share, get a higher limit; checking an address against those lists stores nothing new. For each hour in which the limit turned requests away, the server logs how many it turned away, from how many IP addresses, and the five IP addresses turned away most with their counts, so we can see who runs into the limit and set it right. The daily usage statistics count these refusals without IP addresses.
  • Mouser and DigiKey lookups: these use daily limits shared by every user. For each hour with lookups, the server logs how many were sent or held back by our limits and from how many IP addresses, with the five addresses that sent the most and every address that was held back, so we can spot abuse of the shared limits. It also logs each address once on the day it reaches the per-address daily limit, with the time. To keep those limits fair, a count of the day's lookups per address is kept in memory until the UTC day ends; when the server restarts, the counts of addresses that made at least 100 lookups that day are written to a private file on disk, each under a keyed hash of its address with the key in the same file (not the address itself), and read back and deleted when it starts again. The shared daily counts, and any pause after a distributor refuses requests, are kept on disk so a restart doesn't reset them; they contain no IP addresses, queries or part numbers.
  • Server logs: our web server keeps no request log. The application's log records each request's time, path and result, but sees only our proxy's address, not yours; your IP address appears in it only in the hourly Mouser and DigiKey and rate-limit lines above (our short datasheet links put the part number in the path). It is capped at about 30 MB, with the oldest entries overwritten first, and starts empty with each new release. Its hourly lines, warnings and errors are also kept in one file per UTC day. Each file becomes due for deletion 30 days after that day ends; deletion is checked hourly and at startup or log rotation. The persistent file has a 50 MB daily byte budget; excess lines are dropped and counted. Each record's message, including any traceback, is limited to 2,000 characters in that file.

3. App name

When an MCP client connects, it reports its own name, for example claude-ai or cursor. We count connections per app name per day, and calls and outcomes per app name and tool per day for every tool. A call doesn't carry the app's name, so the server remembers the name each IP address (for IPv6, its /64 block) last connected with, in memory only, until that address has made no connection or tool call for about 10 minutes. These counts are private: per-tool app counts last 30 days; app connection counts and the Mouser, DigiKey and lifecycle per-app counts last 365 days. In individual call records, an app name is kept only if it is a well-known client or has been used from at least 10 different caller addresses that UTC day when the records are written; other names become other, and an unknown app has no name. Well-known clients use a fixed spelling; other retained names use lowercase with normalized spaces, both for the threshold and in the record. For this threshold, all AI-platform addresses count as one caller, and other IPv6 addresses are grouped by /48.

4. Private usage statistics and diagnostic records

We keep daily counts so we can see which tools are useful and which ones fail. For each day, we keep calls per tool and their outcome (found, similar parts only, nothing found, invalid request, rate-limited, failed), the average response time, response-time buckets (excluding invalid and rate-limited calls), calls per hour (and, separately, failed calls per hour), Mouser and DigiKey lookup counts, for failed, empty or invalid calls a reason chosen from fixed labels, server starts by version, and totals for how the checks behind empty searches turned out. We also count the requests our server sent to JLCPCB, LCSC, EasyEDA, SamacSys and DigiKey and the answers our caches gave instead. For these counted requests, we also keep results by type, such as HTTP status group, timeout, connection failure or challenge. We count how many part numbers were asked for with Mouser and DigiKey (including ones our shared limits turned away) and how many of them were different, how many part numbers we didn't send to them because no distributor could list them (a value such as "10k", or an LCSC code), how many requests the per-IP rate limit turned away, per day and per hour (for the days version 0.10.0 ran, also the requests a since-removed busy-server limit turned away), and how part lookups by LCSC code were answered (from our daily snapshot, live from JLCPCB, or from the snapshot when JLCPCB didn't answer). These counts are private and contain no IP addresses or individual search or lookup records. The per-tool app counts and response-time buckets are kept for 30 days. App connection counts and the Mouser, DigiKey and lifecycle per-app counts (distributor_apps) are kept for 365 days, as are other daily counts. If an unreadable stats file is moved aside as usage_stats.json.corrupt, that backup is deleted once its last modification is more than 30 days old, checked at startup and hourly. Our own usage dashboard, on the same server behind a password, keeps a copy of these daily counts indefinitely.

We also count normalized search words, once per word per call, keeping only words used by at least 10 different callers that UTC day. All addresses in the AI platforms' ranges count as one caller, and other IPv6 addresses are grouped by /48 (the same first 48 bits). Only the top 200 qualifying words per day are saved, for 30 days. These can include part-number tokens; we don't save whole phrases in these counts. Words below the threshold are held only in memory for that day's checks. A restart keeps qualifying counts already saved but clears the evidence for words that have not reached the threshold.

To count distinct callers, we hash each IP address with a random salt that is held in memory only. To count how many different part numbers were asked for with Mouser and DigiKey, we hash each part number the same way, with salts of their own. Each salt and its hashes are replaced the first time they are needed on a new UTC day, and a restart discards them too. The per-word and per-app caller checks also use hashes with separate random salts, new each UTC day and after a restart. App hashes can remain in memory for delayed call-record writes. None of these hashes or salts is written to disk; only the counts and qualifying popular words are saved in the usage statistics.

Call records: for 30 days, we keep each recorded call's UTC time to the second, tool, outcome, reason label, duration, app name under the rule above, whether the request came from an AI-platform address range, and the request's shape: which arguments were set, batch size, input form, fixed options and catalog category, subcategory, package and spec names. These records never contain an IP address, search text, part number or caller ID. Requests refused by our rate limiter are counted per minute and reason, with the AI-platform flag, rather than recorded individually. Recording is capped at 1,000,000 rows per UTC day and a memory buffer limited to 20,000 rows and 8 MiB of accounted row memory, including writes in progress; excess rows are dropped. Lists in the shape keep at most 20 argument names, 10 packages and 20 spec names, with counts for omitted entries. These files are private and aren't served over HTTP.

Failed-search text: for 7 days, a separate private store keeps text from searches and lookups that found nothing or failed, including individual missed or failed rows in an otherwise successful batch. We normalize the text, limit it to 200 characters and combine repeats into a count for each tool, reason and text. At most 5,000 distinct entries are kept per UTC day; after that, existing entries still gain counts but new ones aren't kept. Invalid requests, live stock checks labelled stock_below_minimum (the requested LCSC code was found below the requested stock), pages past the end of results, and lookups not sent because the input wasn't a usable part number are excluded. Other empty searches with text, including those blocked by stock or other filters such as blocked_by_min_stock, are recorded. Each entry has only the date, tool, reason, text and count, with no IP address, app name or link to a caller. These files aren't served over HTTP.

5. The website

The website uses no cookies, no analytics, and no tracking scripts. Its fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, so your browser connects to Google when you open a page; Google's handling of that connection is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

6. Email

If you email us, we keep your message and email address so we can reply, and for as long as needed to handle your request.

What we don't collect

We don't ask for names, accounts, passwords, payment details, location, device identifiers, health data, cookies, or chat history.

Who we share data with

We don't sell personal information or share it for advertising. To answer some tool calls, our server makes requests to these parts and CAD data providers. Those requests come from our server. They contain only what that call needs (part numbers, search text, filters, and the provider's own part or model IDs), never your IP address or anything else that identifies you, unless it is in the search text itself:

  • JLCPCB and EasyEDA for live stock, pricing, part details and footprints
  • LCSC for specifications and datasheets when JLCPCB lists none
  • Mouser Electronics and DigiKey for part lookups
  • SamacSys ComponentSearchEngine for ECAD models and KiCad files

Our answers also contain links, such as datasheets, LCSC product pages, manufacturers' datasheets and reference-board sources on GitHub. Our short datasheet links (pcbparts.dev/mcp/datasheet/…) redirect to the PDF on JLCPCB's servers or, when JLCPCB has none, to the manufacturer's datasheet or LCSC's PDF. Opening any of these links connects your browser or AI assistant directly to that site, which then sees the address of whatever opened it: your own, or your AI app's server.

Each provider handles the requests it receives under its own privacy policy. Our server runs on a rented virtual server whose hosting provider can technically access the machine. We may also disclose information if the law requires it.

Your AI assistant (for example Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor) is run by a separate company, and its own privacy policy covers your conversations with it.

Security

All traffic to pcbparts.dev uses HTTPS. The server keeps private usage counts, diagnostic records and cached parts data as described above. API keys for third-party services stay on the server and are removed from any error message before it is sent back to you.

Your choices and rights

Using the service is optional and needs no account, and you can stop at any time by removing or turning off PCB Parts in your AI app. Through ChatGPT or another hosted AI app, the address we see is the app's server, so we can't tell your calls apart from other users'. Because we don't link what we keep to names or accounts, we usually can't find data that belongs to a particular person. If you'd like to ask about, see, or delete data about you, for example log lines containing your IP address, email us at info@pcbparts.dev and we'll do what we can. Depending on where you live, you may have rights under laws such as the GDPR or CCPA, and you can also complain to your local data protection authority.

To keep everything on your own infrastructure, you can self-host the server.

Children

The service is a tool for electronics design. It isn't directed at children under 13 (or under 16 where local law sets that age), and we don't knowingly collect their personal information.

Changes

If we change this policy, we'll update the effective date above. Every change is recorded in the project's public change history.
