Privacy Policy

Effective November 18, 2026

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no cookies, no ads, no analytics trackers.
  • We use what your AI assistant sends a tool (search text, part numbers, filters) to answer that call. Usage counts don't include it, and nothing we keep links it to you.
  • Your IP address is used for rate limiting and abuse prevention. Our logs keep it only in hourly summaries of Mouser and DigiKey lookups and of requests turned away by our rate limit or because the server was busy, and it is never stored in our usage statistics.
  • To answer some calls our server sends part numbers or search text, never your IP address, to JLCPCB, LCSC, EasyEDA, Mouser, DigiKey or SamacSys. Links in our answers, such as datasheet links, take whoever opens them straight to those sites.
  • We don't sell or share data for advertising, and we don't use it to train AI models.

Who we are and what this covers

PCB Parts MCP is an independent, open-source project. This policy covers the hosted service we run: the website at pcbparts.dev and the MCP server at https://pcbparts.dev/mcp, including when you use it through a connector in Claude, Cursor or any other MCP client.

The source code is public, so you can check everything described here. If you or your organization run your own copy of the server, that copy is not covered by this policy; whoever runs it is responsible for it.

What we collect and why

1. Tool call contents

When your AI assistant calls one of our tools, it sends the arguments for that call: for example a search query such as 10uF 0805 capacitor, a part number such as C2040 or STM32F103C8T6, filters, or a board or sensor name. We use them only to answer that call. We never receive your conversation, your files, or your AI assistant's memory, only the arguments your assistant chooses to send. Please don't put personal information in part searches.

Parts of some answers are cached to keep the service fast and to stay within our suppliers' rate limits. Caches are keyed by the part number or search text and contain no IP address or other identifier:

  • JLCPCB, LCSC and EasyEDA answers are reused for up to 24 hours. They are held in memory only, so a server restart clears them.
  • Mouser and DigiKey answers (stock, prices and lifecycle) are stored on our server's disk by part number and reused for up to 3 days (a day when the part wasn't found). They hold only the part numbers asked and the distributors' answers.
  • Lifecycle statuses that Mouser and DigiKey state (for example active, not recommended for new designs, or obsolete) are stored on disk by manufacturer and part number for up to 90 days (30 days when a distributor doesn't list the part, a week when it states no usable status), and used to answer anyone who asks about that part. Each holds the part number, the manufacturer, the status, the distributor's wording and when it was checked.
  • For each manufacturer, we keep per day which of its parts' lifecycle lookups Mouser and DigiKey didn't list and which they did, for 30 days, so we stop asking a distributor about a manufacturer it doesn't carry. These records hold only the manufacturer's name, the part numbers and the distributors' answers: nothing about who asked.
  • SamacSys ComponentSearchEngine search text and results, and KiCad files, are stored on our server's disk and reused for up to one year (a week for a search where some parts had no model). Expired entries are deleted the next time the server starts.
  • EasyEDA answers about whether a part has a footprint are stored on disk with no fixed end date, since they rarely change. Each holds the part number, the answer, EasyEDA's symbol and footprint IDs, and when it was checked.
  • EasyEDA footprint pad positions, used to compare a replacement part's footprint with the original's, are stored on disk by footprint ID with no fixed end date, since footprints rarely change.

Storing these answers means the same lookup doesn't have to be repeated.

If a lookup fails, the error log line may include the part number or search text involved, so we can fix the problem.

2. IP address

  • Rate limiting: we count requests per IP address (for IPv6, per /64 block, the addresses that share their first 64 bits) over a rolling one-minute window, in memory only, so one caller can't overload the shared service. Addresses in the AI platforms' published address ranges (Anthropic's and OpenAI's public lists), which many of their users share, get a higher limit; checking an address against those lists stores nothing new. For each hour in which the limit turned requests away, the server logs how many it turned away, from how many IP addresses, and the five IP addresses turned away most with their counts, so we can see who runs into the limit and set it right. When the server is too busy to take more tool calls, it turns new ones away for a few seconds, and logs a line of the same kind (count, number of IP addresses, the five IP addresses turned away most with their counts) for each hour that had any. The daily usage statistics count both kinds of refusal without IP addresses.
  • Mouser and DigiKey lookups: these use daily limits shared by every user. For each hour with lookups, the server logs how many were sent, from how many IP addresses, and the five IP addresses that sent the most with their counts, so we can spot abuse of the shared limits. The day's lookup counts, and any pause after a distributor refuses requests, are kept on disk so a restart doesn't reset them; they contain no IP addresses, queries or part numbers.
  • Server logs: our web server keeps no request log. The application's log records each request's time, path and result, but sees only our proxy's address, not yours; your IP address appears in it only in the hourly Mouser and DigiKey, rate-limit and server-busy lines above (our short datasheet links put the part number in the path). The application's logs are capped at about 30 MB, with the oldest entries overwritten first, and start empty with each new release.

3. App name

When an MCP client connects, it reports its own name, for example claude-ai or cursor. We count connections per app name per day. These counts are published on our public statistics page.

4. Anonymous usage statistics

We keep daily counts so we can see which tools are useful and which ones fail. For each day, we keep calls per tool and their outcome (found, similar parts only, nothing found, invalid request, rate-limited, failed), the average response time, calls per hour, Mouser and DigiKey lookup counts, for failed or empty calls a reason chosen from a fixed list of labels, and totals for how the checks behind empty searches turned out. We also count the requests our server sent to JLCPCB, LCSC, EasyEDA and SamacSys and the answers our caches gave instead, how many part numbers were asked for with Mouser and DigiKey (including ones our shared limits turned away) and how many of them were different, how many part numbers we didn't send to them because no distributor could list them (a value such as "10k", or an LCSC code), and how many requests the per-IP rate limit turned away. The counts never contain IP addresses, queries or part numbers.

To count distinct callers, we hash each IP address with a random salt that is held in memory only. To count how many different part numbers were asked for with Mouser and DigiKey, we hash each part number the same way, with salts of their own. Each salt and its hashes are replaced the first time they are needed on a new UTC day, and a restart discards them too. The hashes are never written to disk; only the totals are kept. The 30 most recent days of these counts are public at pcbparts.dev/mcp/stats.

5. The website

The website uses no cookies, no analytics, and no tracking scripts. Its fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, so your browser connects to Google when you open a page; Google's handling of that connection is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

6. Email

If you email us, we keep your message and email address so we can reply, and for as long as needed to handle your request.

What we don't collect

No names, accounts, passwords, payment details, location, device identifiers, health data, cookies, or chat history.

Who we share data with

We don't sell personal information or share it for advertising. To answer some tool calls, our server makes requests to these parts and CAD data providers. Those requests come from our server, with our credentials. They contain only what that call needs (part numbers, search text, filters, and the provider's own part or model IDs), never your IP address or anything else that identifies you:

  • JLCPCB and EasyEDA for live stock, pricing, part details and footprints
  • LCSC for specifications and datasheets when JLCPCB lists none
  • Mouser Electronics and DigiKey for part lookups, through their official APIs
  • SamacSys ComponentSearchEngine for ECAD models and KiCad files

Our answers also contain links, such as datasheets, LCSC product pages, manufacturers' datasheets and reference-board sources on GitHub. Our short datasheet links (pcbparts.dev/mcp/datasheet/…) redirect to the PDF on JLCPCB's or LCSC's servers. Opening any of these links connects your browser or AI assistant directly to that site, which then sees your IP address.

Each provider handles the requests it receives under its own privacy policy. Our server runs on a rented virtual server whose hosting provider can technically access the machine. We may also disclose information if the law requires it.

Your AI assistant (for example Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor) is run by a separate company, and its own privacy policy covers your conversations with it.

Security

All traffic to pcbparts.dev uses HTTPS. The server keeps as little data as possible, and what it keeps is mostly anonymous counts and cached parts data. API keys for third-party services stay on the server and are removed from any error message before it is sent back to you.

Your choices and rights

Using the service is optional and needs no account. Because we don't link what we keep to names or accounts, we usually can't find data that belongs to a particular person. If you'd like to ask about, see, or delete data about you, for example log lines containing your IP address, email us at info@pcbparts.dev and we'll do what we can. Depending on where you live, you may have rights under laws such as the GDPR or CCPA, and you can also complain to your local data protection authority.

To keep everything on your own infrastructure, you can self-host the server.

Children

The service is a tool for electronics design. It isn't directed at children under 13 (or under 16 where local law sets that age), and we don't knowingly collect their personal information.
